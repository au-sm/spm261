Prior to 1992

  • Nevada was the only state in the US with full-scale legal sports wagering
  • Prior to 1992, sports betting was not allowed in Atlantic City, NJ.
  • For decades, Atlantic City was the only US location outside Nevada to have legal casinos.
  • 2011–2018: NJ voters and lawmakers repeatedly attempted to legalize sports betting
  • Murphy vs NCAA (2018) — NJ wanted to legalize it vs PASPA prevented it from doing so
  • The Supreme Court addressed the principles of federalism, particularly the Tenth Amendment, which reserves powers not delegated to the federal government to the states.
  • PASPA violated the anti-commandeering doctrine

Sports Betting 101: Odds

Money Line

−350: the odds for favorites have a minus (−) sign in front, and indicate the money you need to risk to win $100

+270: the odds for underdogs will have a plus (+) sign in front, and indicate the money you will win for every $100 risked.

Point Spread

−7: Chiefs must win the game by more than 7 points for you to win your bet.

+7: Eagles must win the game, or lose by less than 7 points for you to win your bet

Total Points

O 54.5: In order to win $100, you must bet $110 on the overall combined score to be higher than 54.5.

U 54.5: In order to win $100, you must wager $110 on the total combined score to be less than 54.5.

● How Bookmakers Profit
20 / 22

How Do Bookmakers Make Money?

  • Bookmakers try to set the line so half the money is bet on each side. If this is the case, the bookmaker cannot lose.
  • Eagles −6 (−110)  ·  Cowboys +6 (−110)
  • −110: the money you need to risk to win $100
  • Bookie pays one bettor $100 and collects $110 from the other ($220 total)
  • Bookmaker wins $10, so the vig is 10/220 = 0.045 = 4.5%
  • Vig — ‘Vigorish’; a typical spread bet (−110) carries around a 4.5% vig.
● How Bookmakers Profit Cont.

Why Odds and Lines Change? Cont.

Sportsbooks like to get the same amount of money on both sides of a bet so they never lose any money.
