— Framing

Why This Joint Proposal Is a Case Study

This past Tuesday, the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, and MLS — with their players’ unions — sent gambling regulators in 35 states and D.C. a joint proposal: lifetime bans for bettors who harass or threaten athletes and their families. The reaction split into two stories — one about enforcement, one about who wrote the enforcement and who got left out of it.

Regulatory Filing

Five Leagues, One Letter, 35 States

  • NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, and MLS — plus their players’ associations — signed the proposal.
  • Sent this past Tuesday to gambling regulators in 35 states and Washington, D.C.
  • Calls for lifetime bans on bettors who harass or threaten athletes or their families.

The Leagues’ Statement

“Threatening the safety of an athlete or their family members over a sports bet crosses a bright ethical and criminal line.”

● The Double-Standard Critique
05 / 12
03
The Revenue Side

The Same Leagues Sell the Bets

  • All five leagues carry official sportsbook partnerships.
  • Betting odds and promos are now built directly into game broadcasts.
  • Sportsbooks already run AI systems to catch the abuse the leagues are now moving to punish.

The Criticism, In One Line

“You created this problem — now you’re punishing the symptom.”

● The Double-Standard Critique Cont.
06 / 12
03
Fan and Media Reaction

Backlash Was Immediate

  • Fans and sports media called the proposal hypocritical within hours of the letter going public.
  • The core objection: leagues aggressively promote the betting behavior tied to the harassment they’re now policing.
  • The proposal punishes bettor conduct without touching the promotional pipeline that leagues built around it.

Discussion

If betting partnerships help fund the leagues now policing bettor behavior, who should own the harassment problem?

A Gap in the Coalition

Two Unions Were Missing From the Letter

  • The WNBA Players Association was not included in the joint letter.
  • The National Women’s Soccer League Players Association was also left out.
  • NWSLPA reportedly learned about the memo only after it had already gone public.

WNBPA Statement

The union said it was “deeply disappointed” at being excluded from the proposal.

● Who Got Left Out Cont.
08 / 12
04
Stakeholder Management

A Safety Policy With an Exclusion Problem

  • Women’s leagues face the same betting-harassment exposure as the men’s leagues that wrote the letter.
  • Leaving two players’ unions out undercuts the leagues’ own stated safety rationale.
  • The gap raises a basic stakeholder question: who gets a seat when the policy is drafted, and who finds out after the fact.

Discussion

What does it mean for a duty-of-care policy to exclude unions representing athletes most exposed to this harassment?

Duty of Care vs. Revenue Model

Where the Betting Business Meets Its Own Policy

The Revenue Model

  • Official sportsbook partnerships
  • In-broadcast betting content
  • League-branded betting products

The Duty-of-Care Move

  • Lifetime bans for harassers
  • Cross-state reporting
  • Shared exclusion lists

The Stakeholder Gap

  • WNBPA excluded
  • NWSLPA excluded
  • Coalition covers 5 leagues, not all

The same institutions that built the betting pipeline are now the ones enforcing its boundaries — and not every stakeholder got a say in where those boundaries sit.

● Synthesis
10 / 12

— Synthesis

Why This Matters for Sport Managers

A

A safety policy can coexist with the revenue model that critics say created the need for it.

B

Cross-league, cross-state coordination is possible when leagues share a legal and reputational interest.

C

Excluding stakeholders from a joint policy creates a visible equity gap, even when the policy itself is defensible.

D

Enforcement mechanisms — bans, shared lists, AI detection — are easier to stand up than fixing the incentive structure underneath.
